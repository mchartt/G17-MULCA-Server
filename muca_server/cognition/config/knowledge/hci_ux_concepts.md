# HCI and UX concepts
Type: support · Version: v1 (2026-10-07) · Source: AUI 2026/27 Lesson 1 (Course Introduction), slides 18–24, 27–28 and 32

## At the crossroad of disciplines (slide 18)
The course sits at the crossroad of computer and information engineering, HCI (Human-Computer Interaction), and design in all its forms, including UX (User eXperience) design.

## User eXperience (slide 19)
A key factor for the success of any modern application is the quality of the User eXperience (UX): how well users interact with a system, and how happy and rewarded they are in doing so. "A good user experience is like an electric light: when it works, nobody notices it; when it does not work well, everybody is disturbed. A good user experience seems obvious, but what is not obvious is how to design, prototype and implement it. Nor is it trivial how to systematically assess such quality, during and at the end of the development process." (R. Hartson, P. S. Pyla, *The UX Book*, Morgan Kaufmann, 2012)

## Human-Computer Interaction (slide 20)
"HCI is concerned with the design, evaluation and implementation of interactive computing systems for human use and with the study of major phenomena surrounding them." (ACM SIGCHI, 1992. ACM = Association for Computing Machinery; SIGCHI = Special Interest Group for Human Computer Interaction.) The user is at the centre of the design and development process.

## HCI key concepts (slide 21)
Key attributes for an interactive technology to be successful:
- **Usability**: do it easily and naturally.
- **Utility**: accomplish what is required.
- **Attractiveness and engagement**: pleasure and fun.
- **Performance and robustness**.
- **Potential for adoption**: people want to "buy" and use it.

Developing high-quality interactive technologies implies: understanding stakeholders and their needs; creating the right design; building the right technology; evaluating UX quality.

Empirical studies ("user studies") are an important branch of HCI: understanding the needs of all stakeholders; analysing their behaviour with technology; understanding how design solutions affect users' perceptions, attitudes and judgements.

## A multidisciplinary field (slides 22–23)
HCI draws on academic disciplines (ergonomics, psychology and cognitive science, design, informatics, engineering, computer science and software engineering, social sciences such as sociology and anthropology), on design practices (graphic, product, artist, industrial design, film industry), and overlaps with interdisciplinary fields (ubiquitous computing, human factors, cognitive engineering, cognitive ergonomics, information systems, computer-supported cooperative work).

Working in multidisciplinary teams means many people from different backgrounds, with different perspectives and ways of seeing and talking about things. Benefit: more ideas and designs are generated. Disadvantage: it can be difficult to communicate and to move the designs forward.

## A switch of perspective (slide 24)
Computer engineering tends to see technology as an end. The HCI perspective sees technology as a means to an end: satisfying users' needs, attitudes and expectations.

## Course goal (slide 27)
Developing the "hard" and "soft" skills needed to create ACCESSIBLE and INCLUSIVE INNOVATION in the field of interactive technologies.

## Innovation (slide 28)
Innovation is the process of translating an idea or invention into a good or service that creates value or for which customers will pay. To be called an innovation, an idea must be replicable (at an economical cost) and must satisfy a specific need. Innovation involves the deliberate application of information, imagination and initiative to derive greater or different value from resources, and includes all processes by which new ideas are generated and converted into useful products. (businessdictionary.com)

## Developing innovation skills (slide 32)
Understanding and anticipating users' needs + thinking out of the box + mastering (the integration of) innovative technologies + supporting the quality of the User eXperience.
