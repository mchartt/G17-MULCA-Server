# Course objectives and learning outcomes
Type: support · Version: v1 (2026-10-07) · Source: AUI syllabus (Programma Dettagliato), "Obiettivi dell'insegnamento" and "Risultati di apprendimento attesi", pp. 2–3

## Course objectives (syllabus p. 2)
Computing devices, digitally enhanced artifacts and artificial intelligence are present in most human activities. Interactive multimedia content is available on personal devices and in physical spaces (large and small displays, projections). Sensors and actuators are embedded in objects, appliances, vehicles, lights and furniture. Wearable headsets offer virtual, augmented and mixed reality experiences. People interact through paradigms that go beyond "click" or "touch": gestures, body movements, tangible manipulation, and text- or speech-based conversation in natural language. AI manages the generation of digital content and the behaviour of the tools we use.

The course gives an overview of the main design methods and technologies for creating innovative interactive solutions that address complex user requirements, particularly for fragile populations and persons with special needs. Through project-oriented activities, students gain competencies in specific technical areas (interactive Internet of Things, virtual/augmented/mixed reality, conversational agents, generative AI) and application domains (health and well-being, education and learning, environmental sustainability).

## DdD1 – Knowledge and understanding (syllabus p. 2)
Students acquire a systematic and critical understanding of goal-oriented requirements engineering methods, UX design principles, and advanced implementation technologies for interactive applications. They gain in-depth knowledge of generative AI tools for multimedia content production and conversational interfaces, and of innovative interaction paradigms: virtual, augmented and mixed reality, full-body interaction, tangible interaction. They also learn empirical research methods and systematic processes for designing, developing and evaluating innovative interactive systems, integrating theory and practice to address complex real-world problems.

## DdD2 – Applying knowledge and understanding (syllabus pp. 2–3)
After the course, students can:
- elicit and analyse requirements that address the salient needs of end users across domains (health, well-being, education, learning, environmental sustainability);
- translate user and system requirements into coherent and effective UX design solutions;
- develop interactive applications that embody innovative interaction paradigms and integrate generative AI components;
- design, conduct and interpret empirical studies that evaluate the quality, usability and real-world benefits of interactive technologies.

## DdD3 – Making informed judgements (syllabus p. 3)
Students develop a critical and reflective attitude toward the potential, limitations and risks of interactive technologies. They learn to choose methods and technologies responsibly for real-world problems, and to evaluate interactive products in terms of human impact, benefits and underlying values, with awareness of ethical, societal and sustainability considerations.

## DdD4 – Communication (syllabus p. 3)
Project work happens in multidisciplinary teams, often with students from different master's programmes, and ends in public presentations. Students learn to:
- summarize and present project outcomes at every stage, from requirements analysis to UX design and implementation;
- produce clear, accurate, well-structured UX design documentation;
- prepare precise, technically sound technology specification documents;
- create compelling video pitches that communicate the application's key features and value to diverse audiences.

## DdD5 – Learning skills (syllabus p. 3)
Students develop the autonomy to keep their knowledge of emerging interactive technologies up to date, engage critically with new developments, face the challenges of innovative solutions, and conceive new applications in a largely self-directed way.
