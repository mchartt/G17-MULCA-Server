# Topics, learning approach, prerequisites and bibliography
Type: support · Version: v1 (2026-10-07) · Source: AUI syllabus (Programma Dettagliato), "Argomenti trattati", "Prerequisiti", "Bibliografia", pp. 3–4

## The three subject areas (syllabus p. 3)
1. **The development cycle of interactive applications**: the main tasks in creating an interactive application and the methods to perform them. Requirements elicitation (needs- and goal-oriented techniques); design (scenario-based approaches); prototyping (sketching approaches).
2. **"Beyond the desktop" interaction paradigms and their enabling technology**: tangible interaction (physical manipulation of "smart", interconnected physical objects enhanced with sensors and actuators); full-body interaction in smart spaces; interaction in virtual, augmented and mixed reality (headsets such as Samsung Gear VR or Microsoft HoloLens); conversational interaction (generative AI solutions such as Large Language Models). Each paradigm is discussed from several perspectives: rationale, vision and design principles; up-to-date implementation technology and off-the-shelf development tools.
3. **Experimental research in Human-Computer Interaction**: research questions; dependent and independent variables; between- vs within-group designs; qualitative vs quantitative data; data gathering techniques (observation, surveys, interviews, focus groups, automated data collection); data analysis; reliability and validity.

## Learning approach (syllabus p. 3)
- **Project-based**: students learn by doing a project.
- **Problem-based**: project work starts from real-world case studies with complex user needs and requirements.
- Project activities benefit from collaborations with educational and therapeutic institutions, companies and research labs; domain experts from these partners may help students understand users' needs and give feedback on results.
- Learning materials are on WeBeep: course slides and videos, and scientific papers on specific topics of the course.

## Prerequisites (syllabus p. 4)
Depending on the project topic, competences in UX design, web design, JSON, JavaScript, C#, Unity and basic electronics are "useful to have", but not strictly mandatory.

## Bibliography (syllabus p. 4)
- Jonathan Lazar, Jinjuan Heidi Feng, Harry Hochheiser, *Research Methods in Human-Computer Interaction*, Elsevier Science & Technology, 2017.
- Jennifer Preece, Helen Sharp, Yvonne Rogers, *Interaction Design*, Wiley, 2015 (http://www.id-book.com/).
