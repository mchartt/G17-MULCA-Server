# Interaction paradigms, methods and project areas
Type: support · Version: v1 (2026-10-07) · Source: AUI 2026/27 Lesson 1 (Course Introduction), slides 25, 34–36, 40–42 and 49

## Advanced interaction paradigms (slide 25)
Interacting not only with digital devices and digital content, but also with objects and physical spaces; and not only with mouse or touch, but also with natural language (text, speech) and the whole body.

## Course content (slide 34)
- The interactive technology development cycle.
- User requirements management: understanding users' needs, representing them, and transforming them into product specifications.
- Interaction modes "beyond the desktop" (advanced interaction paradigms): technologically challenging, not yet widely adopted in everyday life, not fully understood, in some cases uncharted territory (e.g. interactive climbing).

## Interaction paradigms and technologies considered in the course (slide 35)
1. Conversational interaction (in natural language, using speech and text).
2. (Shared) virtual reality interaction.
3. Full-body interaction (in smart multisensory spaces).
4. Tangible interaction (with smart objects).
5. Interaction based on head, eye, mid-air gesture and hand-controller tracking (in XR).
6. Multimodal interaction: a combination of multiple interaction paradigms.

## Methods, technology and evaluation (slide 36)
- **Design methods**: scenario-based design ("stories" of what the user can do with your product); conversational design.
- **Technology**: off-the-shelf implementation tools and devices for the different interaction paradigms; LLMs and generative AI; prompt engineering.
- **Empirical evaluation**: evaluating the quality of the user experience, the benefits of the technology for end users, and its potential for adoption.

## Project theme dimensions (slides 40–41)
Every project theme combines four dimensions:
- **"User" population**: children and adults with learning needs and/or special needs in the emotional or cognitive sphere; their caregivers; scientists and researchers; the general public.
- **General needs**: learning; well-being; therapeutic assessment; therapeutic treatment; autonomy; …
- **Contexts of use**: home, school, therapeutic centre (including hospital), urban transportation, museum, research lab, office…
- **Interaction paradigms and technologies**: X-Reality; cyber-physical systems and IoT; LLMs; AI; machine learning; audio analysis; …

## Project areas: interaction paradigms (slide 42)
- LLM-based (multiparty) conversational interaction (LLM = Large Language Model).
- LLM-based generative AI for educational or therapeutic multimedia content production and/or everyday-life support.
- Tangible interaction.
- Full-body interaction.
- Emotional interaction.
- (Interaction in XR – virtual, augmented, mixed reality: *maybe*.)

## Stakeholders (slide 49)
- A **stakeholder** is any person "interested in" your project.
- **Primary stakeholders**: the end users.
- **Secondary stakeholders**: depend on the project. Example: in an application for a fine-arts museum's visitors, the primary stakeholders are the visitors; secondary stakeholders are the museum curator, fine-arts specialists, the museum education team, …
